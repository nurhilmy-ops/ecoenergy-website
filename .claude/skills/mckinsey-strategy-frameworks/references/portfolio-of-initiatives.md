# Portfolio of initiatives — strategy as a set of bets under uncertainty

## What it is and where it comes from
When the future is uncertain, a single five-year plan is a gamble. Run a portfolio of initiatives
across horizons and risk levels, and let the winners earn more capital. The idea is associated with
Lowell Bryan at McKinsey in the early 2000s ("Just-in-time strategy for a turbulent world",
*McKinsey Quarterly*, 2002). It builds on the Three Horizons and on Courtney, Kirkland & Viguerie,
"Strategy under uncertainty", *Harvard Business Review*, Nov–Dec 1997. That article gives the four
levels of residual uncertainty and the three kinds of move: **no-regrets moves, options, big bets**.

## Step 1 — Name the level of uncertainty (Malaysian RE examples)
| Level | Meaning | Examples (re-verify dates in `malaysia-context.md`) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Clear-enough future | One forecast is good enough | Published rules in force, e.g. the CRESS non-firm SAC of 40 sen/kWh under the current guideline |
| 2. Alternative futures | A few discrete outcomes | Whether the Sep 2026 CRESS Acceleration Package (14 sen firm SAC, fixed 10 years for COD by end-2028) is enacted as announced; whether and when LSS7 is announced; whether a client wins its LSS6 package |
| 3. Range of futures | A continuous range | Data-centre load growth in Johor; module and BESS price paths; SMP levels |
| 4. True ambiguity | Can't bound outcomes | Long-run market design (e.g. a future wholesale market or regional power trading at scale) |

Rule of thumb: the higher the level, the more the portfolio should lean on options and no-regrets
moves, and the fewer big bets it can carry.

## Step 2 — Classify each initiative
| Type | Pays off when | EcoEnergy firm examples | Client examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No-regrets** | Whatever the future | BESS dispatch modelling capability (needed for LSS6 hybrids and large SELCO); a conflicts register; reusable lender-grade model templates | Land screening (`kml-terrain-screening`) before any land payment; grid-capacity enquiries |
| **Option** | Keeps upside open, caps downside | One WtE feasibility with a state agency; a teaming agreement for cross-border trade; a Johor presence via a partner, not an office | Land *option* agreements ahead of LSS7; Stage 01 screening (a small fee buys the right to proceed) |
| **Big bet** | Wins big in some futures, loses big in others | Hiring a full BESS engineering team; opening a southern-region office | Committing land rental and grid deposits before an RFP is announced |

EcoEnergy's own staged engagement model (screening → feasibility → transaction) is an option
structure for clients: each stage is a small premium paid to learn before committing. Use that
language with clients; it explains why a "no" at Stage 01 is a good outcome.

## Step 3 — Check balance
- Big bets only where knowledge is **distinctive** (we know more than rivals), never on unfamiliar
  ground.
- Options need **kill criteria and review dates**; without them, pilots quietly eat the budget.
- If everything sits in Horizon 1, it is safe today and the pipeline is empty tomorrow.

## Step 4 — Set review gates
For each initiative: owner, budget, expected payoff date, the result that earns more capital, and
the result that kills it. Review on policy events (RFP announcements, guideline revisions), not just
on the calendar.

## Where it misleads
- Classifying something as an "option" doesn't make it cheap. Count the leadership hours, which
  are a boutique's scarcest capital.
- Uncertainty levels drift. A rumoured round (Level 2) becomes a published RFP (Level 1) overnight,
  and a "fixed" charge can be re-cut. The CRESS SAC was cut in Aug 2025, and a further cut was
  announced in Sep 2026.
  Re-classify on every policy event.
- The framework assumes you can stage commitments. Some tender conditions (bid security, land
  control at submission) force a big bet early. Price that explicitly.
