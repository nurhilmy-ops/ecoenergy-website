# McKinsey 7S — is the organisation aligned to execute?

## What it is and where it comes from
Seven interdependent elements: three "hard" (Strategy, Structure, Systems) and four "soft" (Shared
values, Style, Staff, Skills), with shared values at the centre. It was first published as
Waterman, Peters & Phillips, "Structure is not organization", *Business Horizons* 23(3), 1980,
pp. 14–26, and popularised in Pascale & Athos, *The Art of Japanese Management* (1981) and Peters &
Waterman, *In Search of Excellence* (1982). The infographic credits only Peters and Waterman and
leaves out Julien Phillips. Cite all three.

## When to use it at EcoEnergy
- **Firm level:** before committing to a new horizon (e.g. building a BESS advisory line), after a
  string of lost bids, or when growth beyond the founding team strains delivery.
- **Bid consortium / SPV level:** before an LSS or CRESS bid, test whether the developer, EPC,
  financier and advisor are aligned. Misalignment shows up later as change orders, delayed
  financial close and disputes.
- **Tender level:** a quick 7S check on the proposed project team. Evaluators score key personnel,
  methodology and organisation chart, which are Staff, Skills, Systems and Structure in disguise.

## Method
1. **Score today.** Rate each S from 1 to 5 with people from every level. In a small firm, use
   short interviews rather than a survey. Gaps between levels are the insight.
2. **Define the target.** Describe what each S must look like for the strategy to work (e.g. the
   LSS6 hybrid round needs BESS dispatch modelling as a core Skill, not a bought-in one).
3. **Check the 21 links.** Seven elements make 21 pairs. Mark each green, amber or red for alignment.
4. **Fix the red links**, starting where the gap is widest. One fix usually pulls other S along.

## EcoEnergy-specific tests (firm level)
| S | What "aligned" looks like | Red flag to probe |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Independent, lifecycle advisory; staged go/no-go | Chasing EPC-style or success-fee work that rewards a "yes" |
| Structure | Clear owner per service line; licensed professionals on call (PE/Ir., counsel, tax agent) | Every sign-off escalates to one director; no deputy for lender Q&A |
| Systems | Models reconcile to source rows; quotation→work-order register; conflicts register; QA before issue | No conflicts register, which threatens the independence claim directly |
| Shared values | "Unknowns flagged, never filled"; "if the site is wrong we tell you" | Fee structures that pay more when the answer is "proceed" |
| Style | Partners spend time on lender defence and quality review | Leadership calendar dominated by sales; reviews skipped |
| Staff | Right people in the roles that matter: BEM-registered PE for engineering submissions, modeller, regulatory lead | Critical roles filled by availability, not fit |
| Skills | Distinctive: integrated engineering + finance + regulatory; BESS-inclusive modelling | A new scheme (hybrid LSS6, CRESS firm supply) with no in-house capability |

**Independence is the shared value that everything else must reinforce.** Test every Systems and
Strategy choice against it. A success fee contingent on financial close, or advising a bidder and
then that bidder's lender, makes the firm's "yes" worth less to lenders. That is the value
proposition on the website.

## Consortium / SPV variant
Run the 7S once per partner, then check the cross-partner links that matter most:
- **Shared values:** does the EPC partner's margin motive conflict with the developer's
  minimum-tariff motive? If so, the EPC price must be fixed (lump-sum, back-to-back LDs) before
  the bid tariff is set. Hand the contract terms to `legal-commercial-contracts`.
- **Systems:** do the partners' financial models and assumptions books reconcile? Hand to
  `fin-model-engineer` and `big4-finance-consultant`.
- **Structure:** who decides at bid submission, and is it written into the consortium agreement?
  Hand to `legal-corporate-dd`.

## Where it misleads
- It is descriptive, not prescriptive. It tells you elements are misaligned, not which strategy is
  right.
- There are no weights or causal ordering, and the empirical validation is thin (it was built from
  consulting cases, not tested hypotheses). Treat scores as structured judgement.
- In a small firm, "Structure" and "Staff" are often the same two or three people. Don't invent an
  org-chart problem; look at key-person dependency instead.
- It is a snapshot. Repeat it after a material change (new service line, new partner, lost bid streak).
