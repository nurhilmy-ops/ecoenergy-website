# Three Horizons of growth — run today's business and build tomorrow's

## What it is and where it comes from
Manage three portfolios at once: **H1** extend and defend the core, **H2** build emerging
businesses, **H3** create options for the future. Source: Baghai, Coley & White, *The Alchemy of
Growth* (1999). Two corrections to popular versions: (1) it is not a sequence; all three are
managed concurrently; (2) fixed time frames (e.g. "H3 = 5–12 years") are not part of the original
logic. Steve Blank's HBR critique (2019) argues disruption can now arrive as fast as H1 work, so
speed matters more than the calendar. See `source-notes.md`.

## EcoEnergy horizon map (firm level, starting hypothesis to re-test yearly)
| Horizon | Candidate businesses | Measure by | Fund like | Kill rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** core | Solar scheme advisory: LSS/CRESS/SELCO/ATAP screening & feasibility, tariff-solving models | Fee margin, utilisation, cash collection days, win rate | Annual budget, efficiency targets | Declining margin on reinvestment |
| **H2** emerging | BESS-inclusive modelling and engineering (LSS6 hybrid is mandatory solar+BESS); lender's TA / transaction advisory; CRESS structuring; sukuk structuring support | Revenue growth, repeat clients, references won, unit economics per mandate | Staged capital tied to milestones (e.g. hire #2 BESS modeller after 3 mandates) | Unit economics don't improve with scale |
| **H3** options | Waste-to-energy and small-hydro advisory; cross-border RE trade; carbon/REC advisory; data-centre green-supply structuring; grid services from storage | Assumptions validated, milestones hit | Many small, cheap bets: one study, one partnership, one article | Key assumption proven wrong |

Calibrate with facts from `malaysia-context.md` (e.g. LSS6 hybrid requirement, CRESS SAC period,
data-centre demand). Don't promote a horizon on enthusiasm alone.

**Small-firm adaptation.** "Venture-style many small bets" is unaffordable for a boutique. H3 bets
should cost weeks, not salaries. Examples: a thought-leadership article that tests demand (count
consult requests it generates), a co-authored study with a state agency, a teaming agreement with a
WtE technology firm, or a minority role in one consortium. Graduation evidence is a paying client.

## Client level
For a developer client, map their pipeline: H1 operating LSS assets (optimise availability,
refinance), H2 CRESS and hybrid projects under development, H3 floating, storage-only or
cross-border options. The typical trap is the same: starving H2 and H3 to hit H1 numbers, or
judging H2 projects on H1 metrics (yield on operating assets) before they can scale.

## Run it in one session
1. List every initiative and tag it H1, H2 or H3.
2. Compare budget and leadership hours per horizon. Many find H3 close to zero.
3. Give each horizon its own scorecard; one set of KPIs kills H2 and H3.
4. Set graduation criteria: what proof moves a bet from H3 to H2, and from H2 to H1?

## Where it misleads
- Horizons describe maturity, not attractiveness. Pair with the GE matrix before funding anything.
- Policy-driven markets jump. A single RFP (LSS6 mandating BESS) can move a business from H3 to H1
  in one announcement, so review horizons when policy changes, not only annually.
- Labelling something H3 can become an excuse to never measure it. Set kill rules on day one.
