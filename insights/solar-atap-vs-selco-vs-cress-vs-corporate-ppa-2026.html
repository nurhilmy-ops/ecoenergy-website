Solar ATAP vs SELCO vs CRESS vs Corporate PPA: which solar route fits your factory in 2026?

Independent Advisory By EcoEnergy Consultancy Sdn Bhd Current as of mid-2026 Updated 26 November 2026 ~9 min read

If you run a factory or commercial facility in Malaysia, you have probably noticed two things over the past year: your TNB bill behaves differently under the new tariff structure, and every solar EPC contractor in the Klang Valley wants to sell you a system. What most of them will not do is give you a straight, independent answer to the real question:

Which scheme — Solar ATAP, SELCO, CRESS, or a corporate PPA — actually fits your load profile?

Half the advice still circulating online references NEM and CGPP. Both are gone. NEM closed and has been replaced by Solar ATAP; CGPP's 800 MW quota was fully awarded. If a proposal on your desk mentions either as a live option, that alone tells you how current the advice is.

Here is the 2026 decision tree, with the actual regulatory parameters — because the numbers changed materially in December 2025, and the differences run to millions of ringgit over a 20-year horizon.

Updated 26 November 2026. We have checked the SELCO rules against the Energy Commission's current guideline (GP/ST/No.51/2024 (Pin.1/2026), revised June 2026) and corrected three points: the connection-study threshold is 72 kWac, not 72 kWp; the Commission gives the standby-charge rate as RM12/kWp without a billing period (we had said "per month"); and systems above 1 MWac that were completed and had applied for their licence by 30 June 2026 are exempt from the battery requirement. The new CRESS Acceleration Package analysis covers its 2028 deadline and whether firm supply pays.

Updated 18 November 2026. The Energy Commission's revised CRESS Guidelines (dated 17 November 2026) bring in a CRESS Acceleration Package from 18 November 2026: firm projects that reach their commercial operation date (COD) by 31 December 2028 can opt into a developer SAC of 14 sen/kWh fixed for 10 years, while the green consumer pays a separate charge, which the Energy Commission put at 4 sen/kWh for RP4 and which is reviewed each regulatory period. We have also corrected the size of the August 2025 SAC cut — 20% for firm supply and about 11% for non-firm, not 40%. On GITA, standalone own-use solar under current MGTC rules needs its capex incurred and its application received by 31 December 2026; Budget 2027 proposes a 2027–2030 window, but only for solar integrated with battery storage, pending gazettal.

First, why your electricity bill is the starting point

Since 1 July 2025, Peninsular Malaysia operates under the RP4 tariff structure, which restructured how commercial and industrial customers are charged — including time-of-use (ToU) bands and maximum demand charges. Before comparing solar schemes, you need to know three numbers from your own bills:

  1. Your average monthly consumption (kWh) and how much falls in peak ToU bands
  2. Your maximum demand (kW) — because peak shaving may matter as much as energy offset
  3. Your daytime load shape — solar without storage only offsets what you consume while the sun is up

These three numbers decide which scheme wins for you. There is no universal answer, which is precisely why "which is cheaper" comparisons from installers selling one product should be read with caution.

Option 1: SELCO — self-consumption, maximum independence

What it is: Solar Photovoltaic for Self-Consumption. You generate and consume on-site, with no export to the grid. Ground-mounted and floating installations within your premises are permitted, and the scheme is open to agricultural users as well as C&I.

Who it fits: Factories with strong, stable daytime load — manufacturing plants and 24-hour facilities that can absorb everything the panels produce.

The 2026 rules you must know (announced by the Energy Commission on 31 December 2025, now in its June 2026 guideline)

  • Battery storage is now required only for non-domestic systems exceeding 1 MWac — at least one hour at full output, so a 2 MWac system needs at least 2 MWh, AC- or DC-coupled (cl. 3.6). Under the March 2025 guideline the threshold was 72 kWp. This is the single biggest cost change of the year: for most SME factories under 1 MWac, the battery requirement that would otherwise have applied from 2026 no longer does. Systems above 1 MWac that were completed and had applied for their licence by 30 June 2026 are exempt too (cl. 3.8).
  • Standby charges apply only to non-domestic installations of 1 MWac and above built after 31 December 2024 (cl. 12.1–12.2); the threshold was 1 MWp until the December 2025 revision moved it to an AC rating. The Energy Commission set the rate at RM12/kWp in March 2025 without stating a billing period, so confirm with TNB how it will appear on your bill before you model it. Below the threshold, you are exempt.
  • Installations above 72 kWac need a Connection Assessment Study (CAS), carried out by the consultant who designs the system (cl. 5.7). At or below 72 kWac, a self-assessment study is needed when TNB asks for one (cl. 5.9).
  • Higher-education institutions registered with the Ministry of Higher Education are exempt from both the standby charge and the battery requirement (cl. 3.7).

The catch: Every kWh you generate but don't consume is wasted. If your load dips on weekends or during shutdowns, your effective rooftop solar ROI stretches. And if you are sizing above 1 MWac, the BESS requirement and standby charge must be built into the financial model from day one — we still see proposals that quietly omit both.

Option 2: Solar ATAP — the NEM successor, with export credits

What it is: The Solar Accelerated Transition Action Program, launched under new Energy Commission guidelines released 30 December 2025, with applications open from 1 January 2026. It replaces net energy metering: you consume your own generation first, and export the excess to the grid for bill credits.

Who it fits: Businesses with high daytime usage but an imperfect load match — where some production would otherwise be wasted under SELCO.

The critical difference from old NEM: For non-domestic consumers, export credits are based on the average system marginal price issued by the Single Buyer — not the retail tariff offset that made NEM so generous. Credits also do not roll over indefinitely, so oversizing your system to "farm" export credits is not the play it once was. The contract runs 10 years.

The catch: The system marginal price is a wholesale-linked figure, typically well below your retail rate. Solar ATAP economics are driven by self-consumption first; the export credit is a sweetener, not the business case. Any proposal that models export at retail rates is wrong.

Option 3: CRESS — buying green power through the grid

What it is: The Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme lets your business buy renewable electricity directly from an independent developer, delivered through the TNB grid under a third-party access model. No panels on your roof; the plant sits elsewhere, and you sign a supply agreement.

Who it fits: Large consumers — CRESS is generally the domain of medium- and high-voltage customers — with big green commitments (RE100, customer ESG demands, Scope 2 emissions targets) and either no suitable roof or loads far exceeding what on-site solar can serve. Publicly announced CRESS project plans already total around 4 GW.

The number that decides everything: the System Access Charge (SAC). On top of the negotiated generation tariff, the SAC is charged for grid delivery. PETRA announced the cut to the standard CRESS rates on 29 August 2025, aligned with the RP4 tariff schedule that began on 1 July 2025: 20% for firm supply and about 11% for non-firm. (The "up to 40%" headline figure refers to the separate CREAM community access charge.)

Supply typeOriginal SACCurrent standard SAC (RP4)
Firm output (e.g., solar + battery storage)25 sen/kWh20 sen/kWh
Non-firm output (intermittent)45 sen/kWh40 sen/kWh

Under the CRESS Guidelines (latest revision GP/ST/No.40/2024 (Pin.1/2026), dated 17 November 2026), the standard SAC is fixed for each three-year Incentive Based Regulation (IBR) regulatory period, and a revision can move it by no more than 15%. The current period, RP4, runs to December 2027.

The same revision adds the CRESS Acceleration Package, in effect from 18 November 2026. Firm-supply projects that reach COD by 31 December 2028 can opt in: the developer then pays an SAC of 14 sen/kWh, fixed for 10 years from COD, and the green consumer pays a separate charge, based on its electricity consumption, which the Energy Commission put at 4 sen/kWh for RP4 and which is reviewed each regulatory period. Either way, the SAC trajectory beyond the fixed period remains the key bankability question on every CRESS deal we review.

The catch: Generation tariff + SAC, plus any charge levied directly on you as the green consumer, must beat your blended retail tariff for the economics to work. At 20 sen/kWh firm SAC under standard CRESS — or 14 sen/kWh for the developer plus your separate 4 sen/kWh charge under the Acceleration Package — the arithmetic can be competitive for large high-voltage users. But the consumer charge is reviewed each regulatory period, so model it as a variable, not a constant, and take a hard look at your tariff category rather than a brochure comparison.

Option 4: Corporate PPA — solar on your roof, someone else's capital

What it is: A solar power purchase agreement where a developer installs, owns, and operates the system on your premises, and you buy the output at an agreed rate — the "zero-capex" model heavily marketed across Malaysia.

Who it fits: Businesses that want the SELCO or Solar ATAP benefit without capital outlay or O&M responsibility. Providers typically look for monthly TNB bills of at least RM10,000–20,000.

The catch: These are 15–20 year contracts. The three clauses that determine whether you got a good deal are the tariff escalation mechanism, the termination/buyout schedule, and the performance guarantee. This is where independent review earns its fee many times over — the developer's lawyer drafted the agreement for the developer.

Don't leave GITA on the table

If you own the asset (SELCO, Solar ATAP, or an outright purchase rather than a PPA), the Green Investment Tax Allowance (GITA) can materially improve your rooftop solar ROI — but the claim windows and qualifying-asset timing under the rules of the Malaysian Green Technology and Climate Change Corporation (MGTC) have tripped up more than one client who installed first and asked questions later. Timing your capitalisation against the incentive window is a design decision, not an afterthought.

Under the current rules, own-use solar sits in GITA Asset Tier 2: a 60% allowance on qualifying capex, set off against up to 70% of statutory income. MGTC's April 2024 guideline requires the capex to be incurred between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2026, and covers applications MGTC receives up to that date. You can only apply once the system is commissioned and its generation approval or licence is issued. Plan for purchase, commissioning and application all to land by 31 December 2026, unless MGTC confirms otherwise in writing.

Budget 2027, tabled on 9 October 2026, proposes extending GITA to 31 December 2030 — but standalone solar is not on the proposed list. Under the proposal, from 2027 solar would qualify only as part of an own-use renewable energy system integrated with battery storage (BESS/ESS), in GITA Asset Tier 1: a 100% allowance, set off against up to 70% of statutory income. The proposal is pending a gazetted exemption order and new MGTC guidelines, so treat it as upside, not the base case. We cover the details in GITA for solar: the timing traps that sink tax claims.

The honest summary

Stable daytime load, system under 1 MWacSELCO — BESS mandate and standby charge no longer apply
High daytime load with excess generationSolar ATAP — but model exports at system marginal price, not retail
Large MV/HV load, ESG-driven, no roof capacityCRESS — run the numbers at 20/40 sen SAC (standard CRESS, RP4) against your tariff category; if the project can be firm and reach COD by 31 December 2028, also run them at the Acceleration Package's 14 sen/kWh developer SAC plus your separate charge (4 sen/kWh for RP4; reviewable each regulatory period)
Good load, no capital appetiteCorporate PPA — get the contract independently reviewed

Every one of these routes can be the right answer. Every one can also be mis-sold. The scheme parameters above are current as of mid-2026, with CRESS and GITA updated to 18 November 2026 — and if the past 18 months proved anything, it is that they move.
